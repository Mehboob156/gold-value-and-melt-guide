# Gold Melt Value Guide

A practical reference for understanding gold purity, weight, spot prices, melt value, and the factors that affect the value of gold jewelry and scrap.

## What Is Gold Melt Value?

Gold melt value is the estimated value of the recoverable gold contained in an item based on its weight, purity, and the current gold spot price.

It is useful as a starting point when evaluating jewelry, scrap gold, coins, or other gold items.

Melt value should not be confused with the retail value of a finished piece or the amount a buyer will actually pay. A buyer may offer less than the theoretical melt value because of refining costs, testing, operating expenses, risk, and profit margins.

## Understanding Gold Purity

Gold purity is commonly expressed using karats.

Pure gold is treated as 24K. Lower-karat gold contains gold mixed with other metals.

The basic relationship is:

```text
Gold purity = Karat ÷ 24
```

For 14K gold:

```text
14 ÷ 24 = 0.5833
```

So 14K gold contains approximately:

```text
58.33% gold
```

The remaining portion consists of other metals used to create the desired hardness, durability, color, or other characteristics.

### Common Gold Purities

| Karat | Approximate Gold Content |
| ----- | ------------------------ |
| 10K   | 41.7%                    |
| 14K   | 58.3%                    |
| 18K   | 75.0%                    |
| 22K   | 91.7%                    |
| 24K   | 99.9%+                   |

The higher the karat, the greater the proportion of gold in each gram.

## How 14K Gold Price Per Gram Is Calculated

Gold is commonly quoted internationally by the troy ounce, while jewelry is often weighed in grams.

One troy ounce contains:

```text
31.1035 grams
```

The approximate 14K gold value per gram can therefore be calculated as:

```text
14K value per gram =
(spot price per troy ounce ÷ 31.1035) × (14 ÷ 24)
```

Or:

```text
14K value per gram =
(spot price ÷ 31.1035) × 0.5833
```

### Example

Suppose the gold spot price is:

```text
$4,000 per troy ounce
```

First calculate the pure-gold value per gram:

```text
$4,000 ÷ 31.1035 ≈ $128.60
```

Then apply the 14K purity:

```text
$128.60 × 0.5833 ≈ $75.02
```

The estimated 14K melt value would therefore be approximately:

```text
$75.02 per gram
```

This is only an example. Gold prices change continuously, so the result depends on the spot price used.

## Calculating the Melt Value of a Gold Item

Once the approximate value per gram is known, the basic calculation is straightforward:

```text
Melt value = gold weight × value per gram
```

For example, if an item contains 8 grams of 14K gold and the calculated 14K value is $75.02 per gram:

```text
8 × $75.02 = $600.16
```

The theoretical melt value would be approximately:

```text
$600.16
```

However, this calculation assumes that the entire 8 grams consists of 14K gold.

If the item contains gemstones, steel components, springs, clasps, or other non-gold materials, those components should be considered before estimating the actual gold content.

## Gold Weight Conversions

Different buyers and gold markets may use different weight units.

### Troy Ounce

Gold is normally priced using the troy ounce rather than the ordinary household ounce.

```text
1 troy ounce = 31.1035 grams
```

### Pennyweight

Pennyweight, abbreviated as `dwt`, is another unit frequently used when weighing precious metals.

```text
1 dwt = 1.55517 grams
```

Therefore:

```text
20 dwt = 1 troy ounce
```

### Regular Ounce

A standard avoirdupois ounce is:

```text
1 regular ounce = 28.3495 grams
```

It is important not to confuse a regular ounce with a troy ounce when calculating gold value.

## Spot Price, Melt Value, and Buyer Offer

These three concepts are related but different.

### Gold Spot Price

The spot price represents the market price used as the basis for valuing gold, commonly quoted per troy ounce.

### Melt Value

Melt value estimates the value of the actual gold content in an item using its weight and purity.

### Buyer Offer

A buyer's offer is the amount they are willing to pay for the item.

The offer can be lower than the calculated melt value because the buyer may need to cover:

* Testing
* Refining
* Processing
* Transportation
* Business overhead
* Market risk
* Profit margin

A jewelry item may also have additional value because of its design, brand, craftsmanship, age, or gemstones.

For this reason, melt value is best used as a **reference point**, rather than as a guaranteed selling price.

## How to Check Whether Jewelry Is 14K

Gold jewelry may contain markings that indicate its karat or fineness.

Common markings associated with 14K gold include:

```text
14K
14KT
585
```

A `585` marking represents approximately 58.5 parts gold per 1000.

The mathematical 14K calculation is based on:

```text
14 ÷ 24 = 58.33%
```

Markings can help identify an item, but a stamp by itself does not establish the exact weight, condition, or amount a buyer will pay. Testing may be appropriate when the value of an item is significant.

For a more detailed reference, see the [Gold Hallmark Guide](https://14kgoldpricepergram.com/gold-hallmarks-guide/).

## Solid Gold, Gold-Filled, and Gold-Plated Items

Not every item carrying a gold-related marking should be valued as solid karat gold.

### Solid 14K Gold

A solid 14K item is made from a 14K gold alloy throughout the relevant gold components.

### Gold-Filled

Gold-filled products contain a layer of karat gold bonded to another metal. Their total weight should not automatically be treated as the weight of solid 14K gold.

### Gold-Plated

Gold-plated products have a layer of gold over another material. The amount of recoverable gold can be much smaller than the item's total weight.

Examples of markings can include:

```text
14K GF
1/20 14K
GP
HGE
RGP
```

The marking and construction of the item should therefore be considered before calculating its value.

---

## Stones and Other Non-Gold Components

A jewelry item can contain much more than gold.

Examples include:

* Diamonds
* Gemstones
* Glass
* Steel springs
* Non-gold clasps
* Decorative materials
* Other metal components

If the entire item is weighed together, using that entire weight as 14K gold weight can overstate the melt value.

For a scrap-gold calculation, the goal is to determine the weight of the gold-bearing material as accurately as possible.

Gemstones and finished-jewelry value should also be considered separately because they are not represented by a basic gold melt calculation.

---

## Comparing Different Karats

Suppose two pieces have the same total weight but different karat ratings.

The higher-karat piece contains a larger proportion of gold.

For example:

```text
10K → 41.7% gold
14K → 58.3% gold
18K → 75.0% gold
22K → 91.7% gold
```

This means karat is an essential part of any gold-value calculation.

Weight alone is not enough.

## How to Estimate the Value Before Selling

A simple process can make a gold-selling decision more informed.

### 1. Identify the Purity

Look for markings such as 10K, 14K, 18K, 22K, or 24K.

### 2. Weigh the Item

Use a sufficiently precise scale and record the weight in grams when possible.

### 3. Separate Non-Gold Components

Consider gemstones and other materials that should not be counted as gold.

### 4. Check the Current Gold Price

Use a current spot price rather than an old price from a previous transaction.

### 5. Calculate the Melt Value

Apply the appropriate purity percentage to the gold price and weight.

### 6. Compare Multiple Offers

If you are selling, obtaining several quotes can help you understand how a particular offer compares with the theoretical melt value.

### 7. Ask How the Buyer Calculated the Offer

A useful question is whether the buyer is paying based on the tested karat, actual gold weight, and current market price.

## A Simple Calculation Workflow

The entire process can be represented as:

```text
Gold spot price
       ↓
Convert troy ounces to grams
       ↓
Apply karat purity
       ↓
Calculate value per gram
       ↓
Determine actual gold weight
       ↓
Calculate estimated melt value
       ↓
Compare with buyer offers
```

This approach separates the mathematical value of the metal from the commercial value of the finished item.

## Why Gold Prices Change

Gold prices can move significantly over time.

Factors affecting the market can include:

* Supply and demand
* Currency movements
* Interest rates
* Inflation expectations
* Investment demand
* Central-bank activity
* Broader economic conditions

Because of these changes, a gold-value calculation should use a price appropriate to the date and time being evaluated.

## Practical Example

Consider a hypothetical 14K gold ring weighing 6 grams.

Assume the calculated 14K value is:

```text
$75.02 per gram
```

The theoretical melt value would be:

```text
6 × $75.02 = $450.12
```

If the ring contains a gemstone or other non-gold component, the full 6 grams should not automatically be treated as gold weight.

Likewise, a buyer may offer less than $450.12 because the buyer's business has testing, refining, operating, and resale costs.

The calculation therefore provides a **benchmark**, not a guaranteed offer.

## Key Takeaways

The most important principles are:

1. **Karat determines gold purity.**
2. **14K gold contains approximately 58.33% gold.**
3. **Gold is commonly priced by the troy ounce.**
4. **One troy ounce equals 31.1035 grams.**
5. **Melt value is based on gold content, not simply the total weight of an object.**
6. **Stones and non-gold components can affect the calculation.**
7. **Melt value and an actual buyer offer are not necessarily the same.**
8. **Current gold prices should be used for current valuations.**
9. **Comparing multiple offers can provide a better picture of the market.**
10. **Finished jewelry may have value beyond its raw gold content.**
